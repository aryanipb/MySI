Rooftop solar adoption doubles in Pune as subsidies reach households

By Meera Kulkarni
2 October 2026
Share on X WhatsApp

Household rooftop solar installations in Pune doubled over the past year, according to figures released by the state electricity distributor on Tuesday, as a central subsidy scheme finally reached ordinary homes.

The distributor said 18,400 homes in the Pune circle had connected rooftop systems by the end of September, up from 9,150 a year earlier. Officials attributed most of the growth to faster approvals, a simpler online application, and the subsidy, which covers up to 40 percent of the cost of a three-kilowatt system.

Why households are switching

Residents who spoke to the Herald cited rising tariffs, frequent summer outages, and the promise of lower bills. “Our bill dropped from about ₹3,200 a month to under ₹600 after the panels went up,” said Anil Deshpande, a retired engineer in Kothrud.

Installers say the typical payback period, the time it takes for savings to cover the cost, has fallen to around four years for homes that use most of their power during the day.

  • Average system size: 3.2 kilowatts
  • Average cost after subsidy: ₹1.1 lakh
  • Average approval time: 21 days, down from 60

Grid concerns remain

Not everyone is convinced the growth can continue at this pace. Engineers at the distributor warned that some neighbourhood transformers, built decades ago, were not designed for power flowing back into the grid, and that upgrades would be needed in older parts of the city.

A spokesperson said the utility had budgeted for 140 transformer upgrades this financial year, and would prioritise areas with the highest number of applications.
